# Sense of Style Writing

Help readers understand the intended meaning with accuracy, ease, and appropriate pleasure. Apply the book's reasoning to the user's genre; do not imitate Pinker's personal voice. These instructions and references are an independent synthesis, not an exhaustive usage manual.

## Establish the writing brief before drafting

Use the conversation and supplied material first. Establish a **writing category**, **audience**, and **desired outcome** before producing new or substantially rewritten prose.

- If the user has already specified them, use them without another intake question.
- If the category is missing or several plausible categories would produce materially different results, ask before drafting. Offer a few relevant categories from [writing categories](references/writing-categories.md), not the entire catalogue every time.
- Bundle missing information into one compact question where possible: “What are we writing, who is it for, and what should readers understand, feel, or do afterward?”
- Wait for answers to material brief questions. Meanwhile, you may inspect sources or diagnose an existing draft. Do not silently choose a category after a timeout.
- Ask about voice, channel, length, or required evidence only when missing information would materially change the result. Avoid a questionnaire for a simple task.
- Carry the brief forward across revisions. A narrow correction or critique of an existing passage does not require restarting intake; use its established category and scope.

Choose a primary category and, when useful, a secondary one. A founder's newsletter explaining an engineering choice may combine personal-brand and explanatory writing. Categories are not interchangeable templates.

## Draft from meaning to presentation

Before polishing, identify the actual point and the information supporting it. Distinguish supplied facts, inference, uncertainty, and recommendation. An unresolved premise needs evidence or clarification, not a more confident sentence.

Select a progression that fits the reader's task: a concrete case developed into a concept, an item moving through a process, or a decision supported by alternatives and evidence. These are options, not mandatory structures. Give the reader enough context to interpret the opening and enough reason to follow the next paragraph.

Draft with specific actors, actions, objects, or relationships where they help. Define technical concepts at the depth this reader needs; retain specialist terms that make real distinctions. Introduce abstract ideas through a usable example when appropriate, then explain the general concept and the limits of the example.

Classic style treats the reader as a capable conversational equal. It can guide an explanation, but a runbook needs practical instructions, a reference needs searchability, and a personal essay may need reflection. Use the category to decide which virtues deserve emphasis.

## Revise while preserving voice

Default: **preserve the author's voice; restructure when clarity requires it**.

Identify distinctive diction, rhythm, humor, formality, perspective, and recurring imagery in the draft or supplied samples. Keep features that serve the piece. Repair obscurity without replacing the author with a uniform professional tone. Preserve deliberate fragments or repetition when they work for the genre.

Change organization when the point is buried, prerequisites arrive late, references become unclear, or the reasoning is hard to follow. Explain material restructuring briefly when delivering a rewrite, unless the user wants only the finished text. Do not narrate routine copyedits.

Match the requested mode:

- **Draft:** produce the requested piece once the brief is established.
- **Revise:** provide the improved text, not merely advice. Respect a requested light-edit scope.
- **Critique:** identify consequential issues with representative repairs; do not replace the whole piece unless asked.
- **Teach:** explain the diagnosis and tradeoff using a short example.

## Preserve meaning and exercise judgment

- Retain factual scope, causal strength, attribution, uncertainty, dates, quantities, and defined technical distinctions. Do not add evidence, credentials, anecdotes, metrics, or personal experience to make prose persuasive.
- Separate a reader's reported confusion from their proposed solution. Feedback is evidence; not every suggested edit is an improvement.
- Do not impose blanket bans on passive voice, jargon, abstraction, nominalizations, long sentences, short paragraphs, sentence-opening conjunctions, or sentence-ending prepositions. Ask what each construction accomplishes here.
- Do not mechanically delete hedges. Replace vague cushioning with an accurate boundary where the evidence permits it.
- Avoid optimizing a readability score, word count, or fixed sentence pattern at the expense of meaning or voice.
- Apply existing user and publication conventions. When a convention conflicts with clear meaning, explain the issue and propose a compatible repair.
- Style work does not authorize changing the underlying argument or researching, publishing, or sending material beyond the task. Follow the host's research and action requirements.

## Finish at the requested scope

Check the reader's point of entry, missing knowledge, progression, references, logical links, sentence grouping, and claim fidelity. Scale this review to the piece: a two-line email does not need a report-length procedure.

Deliver the requested artifact. Add only useful editorial notes or unresolved factual questions. Do not cite Pinker in the user's prose unless the subject calls for it. Stop when the material problems are resolved and the brief is met; do not keep flattening a successful voice through extra polishing.
