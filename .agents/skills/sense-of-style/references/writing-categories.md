# Writing categories

Use this reference to establish the brief and adapt the principles. Ask for missing category, audience, and outcome before drafting, as directed by SKILL.md. If these are already clear from the user's instructions, proceed. A channel is not a complete purpose: a newsletter could teach, persuade, or report.

## Category guide

| Category | Typical deliverables | Success criterion | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explanatory and educational | Nonfiction articles, tutorials, conceptual guides | Readers can explain the mechanism and recognize its limits. | Definitions arrive without examples, or examples without a general principle. |
| Technical documentation | READMEs, API references, runbooks, procedures | Readers can find the relevant information and act correctly. | Attractive prose omits preconditions, exact names, or failure behavior. |
| Business and decision writing | Proposals, strategy memos, reports | Readers can assess or make a decision. | Discussion obscures the recommendation, tradeoffs, or required action. |
| Personal brand and thought leadership | Essays, newsletters, opinion pieces | A specific perspective is recognizable and supported. | Generic confidence replaces the author's actual experience or judgment. |
| Short-form public writing | Social posts, announcements, captions | One useful idea survives the space and attention constraints. | A dramatic hook overstates a modest claim. |
| Professional correspondence | Emails, introductions, requests, feedback | The recipient understands intent and the appropriate next step. | Politeness or background hides the request. |
| Persuasive and marketing writing | Landing pages, product descriptions, sales material | Readers understand the value, its evidence, and the invitation to act. | Benefits are unsupported, vague, or detached from a reader's situation. |
| Research and analytical writing | Literature reviews, evidence summaries, analytical reports | Readers can distinguish what is known, inferred, contested, and unresolved. | Smooth exposition erases methodological or evidential boundaries. |

Broad nonfiction is an umbrella, not a ninth category. Select the more specific purpose when possible. Fiction, poetry, and screenwriting are outside the default scope; do not force them into an expository pattern.

## Explanatory and educational

Start from the audience's actual prerequisite knowledge. For beginners, a concrete case can make the problem legible before naming the concept. For experts, lead with the distinction or result they need. Provide intermediate causal steps that readers cannot reasonably supply themselves.

An explanation should let a reader answer “How does this work?” and “When does this explanation stop applying?” Distinguish an analogy from the actual mechanism. Do not introduce a colorful analogy that requires more explanation than the original subject.

Possible progression: familiar situation, puzzle, mechanism, general principle, boundary. Use another order when the purpose warrants it.

## Technical documentation

Choose between a tutorial, a task procedure, a reference, and a conceptual explanation. They have different reading patterns. A reference should support lookup; a tutorial should establish prerequisites in usable order.

For actionable instructions, preserve exact commands, identifiers, units, and interface labels. Identify relevant starting conditions, actions, expected results, and recovery information. Ask about missing versions or environments when they affect correctness. Do not invent commands or imply that a procedure was tested when it was not.

Headings, repetition of exact names, lists, tables, and explicit sequencing can be essential. Classic style's aversion to excessive signposting is not a reason to remove navigation or necessary instructions.

## Business and decision writing

Establish whether the reader needs a decision, a recommendation, a status update, or a record. Put the decision or status where the reader can find it. Connect alternatives to consistent criteria and reveal the costs or assumptions that could change the recommendation.

When a causal claim is uncertain, distinguish an observation from its proposed explanation. “Revenue rose after the campaign” and “The campaign caused revenue to rise” are different assertions. A polished memo cannot close that evidential gap.

## Personal brand and thought leadership

Identify what this author notices, believes, questions, or has learned. Preserve recognizable voice and intellectual commitments. Use supplied experiences as evidence; do not fabricate first-person stories, audience reactions, achievements, or quotations.

A distinctive position need not be contrarian. State the actual observation and develop its implications. Avoid introducing an opponent merely to make the opening sound forceful. Ask for an example or experience if the proposed piece depends on one that has not been supplied.

## Short-form public writing

Determine the channel and any actual length restriction. Establish one main point, with enough context to prevent misreading. A short sequence may be needed if one post cannot responsibly hold the explanation; do not expand into a thread without a reason grounded in the request.

A hook should pay off in the body. Preserve uncertainty even when compressing. Do not add hashtags, emoji, calls to action, or a stock closing question unless appropriate to the requested voice and channel.

## Professional correspondence

Clarify the relationship, desired action, and any real deadline. Match warmth and directness to that relationship. Make the request identifiable without making every message brusque.

Preserve commitments precisely: willingness to discuss is not acceptance; a proposed date is not a confirmed date. Drafting a message is not permission to send it.

## Persuasive and marketing writing

Connect a specific reader's situation to an understandable benefit. Explain the mechanism or evidence behind meaningful claims. Use concrete proof supplied by the user, and preserve its limits.

A useful progression may be problem, relevant benefit, mechanism, proof, and next step. Do not force this on every product description. Distinguish current capabilities from proposed ones. Never manufacture testimonials, scarcity, urgency, or performance numbers.

## Research and analytical writing

Keep source attribution attached to the right claim. Separate a study's result from an author's interpretation and your synthesis. Preserve population, conditions, measurement, and uncertainty when they materially constrain an inference.

The activity of researchers can be the real topic of a literature review. Do not delete such discussion as “professional narcissism” when it explains a debate or evidential history. In other documents, lead with the phenomenon rather than a chronology of researchers when that better serves the reader.

## Hybrid requests

Choose the primary reader outcome, then incorporate the secondary category. For example, an analytical newsletter can retain a personal opening while applying research standards to its claims. A technical proposal can be decision-oriented at the top and procedural in an appendix.

If the choices pull in different directions, ask about the priority rather than silently compromising both. Do not ask the user to choose a rigid category label when they can describe the intended result more naturally.
