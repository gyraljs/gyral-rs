# Sources and private reference access

## Provenance and scope

This skill is an independent synthesis based on Steven Pinker's *The Sense of Style: The Thinking Person's Guide to Writing in the 21st Century* (2014), informed by a full-text conversion supplied for study and a companion deep dive. It is not an official or endorsed Pinker product. The instructions, category guide, workflows, and worked examples are original adaptations.

The full book and the full companion deep dive are not bundled. Their useful decisions have been reorganized into focused references. The package works without a private copy, network access, or any particular conversion tool. Do not load the entire source book for an ordinary writing task.

## Scientific claims need boundaries

The book uses cognitive research to explain editorial problems. Do not treat every recommendation as independently experimentally proven or every historical finding as a universal current result.

- Working-memory limits motivate reducing unresolved dependencies; they do not imply a universal sentence-length cap.
- Chunking explains why expert compression can exclude novices; it does not make abstraction intrinsically bad.
- Context effects motivate orienting the reader; they do not require every document to start with a summary.
- Negation can impose difficulty depending on scope and context; the account does not justify banning *not*.
- Readers' expertise affects how much connection must be explicit; adding all possible transitions is not a reliable optimum.

For a task about the science itself, consult the original research and distinguish its design and findings from the book's editorial application. A task to edit prose normally does not require a new literature review. Follow the host environment's research requirements when making external claims.

## Optional private source consultation

If the user has supplied a local book or companion guide, consult a targeted portion only when a detailed question exceeds these references, the user asks for source-grounded explanation, or an exact attribution matters. Use the path established in that session. Do not assume a developer's personal path exists on another machine or search unrelated personal folders for a book.

Search by chapter title and then by the relevant concept. Useful phrases include *functional fixity*, *chunking*, *light before heavy*, *given*, *center-embedded*, *coherence relation*, *scope*, *focus*, *restrictive*, and *punctuation*. Retrieve enough surrounding context to capture the qualification or exception. A search hit is not a substitute for reading the passage.

PDF-to-Markdown conversion may interleave comparison columns, omit sentence trees, and misplace footnotes. Verify important visual distinctions against the supplied PDF when needed. Do not infer an author's verdict from the position of text in a damaged table. Use chapter or section references instead of assuming converted line numbers equal printed pages.

Keep a full copyrighted source outside the shareable skill repository. Do not copy it into references, embed it in generated outputs, or add a downloader. If a required source is unavailable, state the narrow limitation and continue with the self-contained guidance where possible; request the relevant passage only when the task depends on it.
